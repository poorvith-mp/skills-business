# Change Management

Structure change so people adopt it, not just so it is announced.

## Process
1. **Establish the case for change.** What breaks if nothing changes? Write it in the terms the affected group cares about, not leadership's terms.
2. **Map who is affected and how.** Per group: what changes in their day, what they lose, what they gain, who they trust for information.
3. **Find the resistance before it finds you.** Most resistance is rational — surfacing a real cost nobody accounted for. Treat it as information, not obstruction.
4. **Sequence the rollout.** Pilot with a group that has capacity and influence. Do not start with the loudest sceptics or the busiest team.
5. **Define adoption metrics up front** — usage, not attendance. Training completion is not adoption.
6. **Build the feedback loop** and respond visibly, or the next round of feedback will not come.

## Deliverables
- Stakeholder impact map with named owners
- Communication plan by group, message and channel
- Training plan tied to what each role actually does differently
- Adoption dashboard with baseline and target

## Verification & Quality Checklist

- [ ] Each step has a named owner, not a team-shaped placeholder.
- [ ] Inputs and outputs defined for every step, including their format.
- [ ] Failure and escalation path defined for the steps that can block.
- [ ] Completion is measurable - someone can tell whether it is done.

## Anti-Patterns & Constraints

- NEVER automate a process before redesigning the parts that are broken.
- NEVER map a current state without input from the people who do the work.
- NEVER hand off a process without naming who owns it next.
